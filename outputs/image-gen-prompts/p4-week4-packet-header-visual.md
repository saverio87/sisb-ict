# Atomic Visual: What's on a packet header?

## Specifications
- **Lesson**: P4, Week 4, Computer Systems
- **Concept**: Packet header as the destination address label
- **Atomic purpose**: After seeing this, students will understand that a packet header is the address label on a packet that tells routers where to send it.
- **What this image is NOT about**: What's inside the packet, how routers read headers, or technical packet structure.
- **Challenge lens**: Abstract entity — "packet header" is jargon that needs a concrete visual anchor.
- **Audience**: P4 (9–10 years old), mixed ability, some EAL learners
- **Aspect ratio**: 4:3 landscape for slides

## Visual description
A close-up view of a single packet (small cardboard parcel box) in the centre of the image. On top of the box is a very large, prominent sticky label (the header). The label is coral-coloured and shows "TO: 192.168.1.5" in bold black text. A small arrow (sky blue) points to the label from above, with the word "header" next to the arrow (this is the only text label in the image, and it's essential to connect the visual to the term). The background is clean white. The packet box has rounded corners and soft shadows.

## Text in image
- "TO: 192.168.1.5" on the label (essential to show what an address looks like)
- "header" with an arrow pointing to the label (essential to connect the visual to the term)

## Style
Friendly flat-vector cartoon style. Bright primary palette: coral (#FF6B6B) for the label, sky blue (#4A90E2) for the arrow, sunny yellow (#FFD93D) for highlights. Clean white background. Rounded shapes, soft shadows, approachable cartoon aesthetic.
