# Atomic Visual: Misconception — data doesn't travel as one big file

## Specifications
- **Lesson**: P4, Week 4, Computer Systems
- **Concept**: Large files are split into packets for faster, more reliable delivery
- **Atomic purpose**: After seeing this, students will understand that large files are broken into smaller packets for faster, more reliable delivery, not sent as one big chunk.
- **What this image is NOT about**: How the splitting happens, what happens during reassembly, or technical packet switching details.
- **Challenge lens**: Misconception — students assume data is sent whole, not split.
- **Audience**: P4 (9–10 years old), mixed ability, some EAL learners
- **Aspect ratio**: 4:3 landscape for slides

## Visual description
Two scenes side by side, divided by a vertical line or clear space. LEFT SCENE (labelled with a red X or "wrong" indicator): One giant cardboard box (much larger than the pipe it's trying to fit through) is stuck, blocked, unable to move through a small pipe or tube. The box looks strained and stuck. RIGHT SCENE (labelled with a green checkmark or "right" indicator): The same giant box has been split into 5 small boxes (packet-sized), and they are flowing easily through the same pipe, one after another, moving smoothly. The small boxes are coral-coloured and fit perfectly. The background is clean white.

## Text in image
None — the visual shows the contrast between stuck (wrong) and flowing (right), no text needed.

## Style
Friendly flat-vector cartoon style. Bright primary palette: coral (#FF6B6B) for the boxes, slate grey (#5A5A72) for the pipe, mint green (#6BCB77) for the "right" indicator, red for the "wrong" indicator. Clean white background. Rounded shapes, soft shadows, approachable cartoon aesthetic.
