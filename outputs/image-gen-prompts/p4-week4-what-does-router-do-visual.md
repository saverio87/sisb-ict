# Atomic Visual: What does a router actually do?

## Specifications
- **Lesson**: P4, Week 4, Computer Systems
- **Concept**: Router function
- **Atomic purpose**: After seeing this, students will understand that a router is a traffic director that reads the destination on a packet and sends it the right way.
- **What this image is NOT about**: How routers connect to the internet, technical specifications, network topology, or what's inside a router.
- **Challenge lens**: Abstract entity — students know the word "router" but don't understand its function.
- **Audience**: P4 (9–10 years old), mixed ability, some EAL learners
- **Aspect ratio**: 4:3 landscape for slides

## Visual description
A friendly cartoon router (a small box with two antennae on top, like a home Wi-Fi router) stands at a crossroads where three paths meet. The router has simple cartoon eyes and is holding up small packets (miniature versions of the parcel boxes from visual 1) in its hands. Each packet has a visible label. The router is pointing one packet down the left path, another down the right path, based on reading the labels. The paths are simple coloured lines (coral, sky blue, mint green) leading off-screen. The background is clean white.

## Text in image
None — the visual shows the action of directing based on labels, no text needed.

## Style
Friendly flat-vector cartoon style. Bright primary palette: sky blue (#4A90E2) for the router body, coral (#FF6B6B) for the packets, mint green (#6BCB77) for one path, sunny yellow (#FFD93D) for highlights. Clean white background. Rounded shapes, soft shadows, approachable cartoon aesthetic.
